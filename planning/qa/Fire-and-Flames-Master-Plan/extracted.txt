Product and scope
Build one trustworthy event platform with two distinct public experiences and one controlled operations system.
Students should understand the organizer, event requirements, price and payment status before registering. Staff
should see only the records and actions their assigned duties require.
Confirmed foundation
Fire & Flames is the E-Fest of Srusti Academy of Management & Technology (Autonomous), organized by ED Cell on
13–14 November 2026. The shared entrance also presents Kurukshetra esports, Free Fire and BGMI, organized by
Srustiprosar per the user's instruction. Dates for individual esports sessions require confirmation. Students from other
colleges are welcome. Teachers have a separate sign-in entry but do not automatically receive management
permissions.

Release scope
The first production release includes both landing paths, the six documented E-Fest activities, esports information
pages, student/teacher authentication, team and solo intake, manual official-QR payment reconciliation, passes, online
scanning, scoped operations, judging, controlled results, notifications and support. Paid esports intake opens only after
its separate configuration is approved. This is a feature gate, not a fake free registration option.
Each event has its own registration. A student can enter multiple compatible activities; there is no implied all-event pass
or combined fee. The initial checkout handles one event registration per order. Team price is charged once per team,

Proposed technology direction
Prefer a separate React and TypeScript application, a managed PostgreSQL backend, managed authentication, private
file storage and server-side operations. Supabase and Vercel are the proposed fit based on Crossfire's existing
approach; versions, account ownership, hosting plan and costs must be assessed at implementation. Do not reuse
Crossfire's live database or keys.
Sites was assessed as an alternative. Its documented hosted identity is ChatGPT sign-in. It is not the selected
production path for the requested college email and teacher/student flow unless the college explicitly accepts that
identity model. No Sites project is registered during planning.
Success measures and limits
Acceptance targets: no unauthorized cross-track access; no duplicate payment allocation; no confirmed entry without
verified funding and valid roster; no duplicate same-session attendance; all operational changes auditable. Target a
typical verified scan response below two seconds on a working campus connection. Provisional load test: 500
concurrent browsing sessions, 50 concurrent registration mutations and 20 scanners; validate actual expected
attendance before sizing hosting.
Public loading should target LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds and CLS at or below 0.1,
measured at the 75th percentile where field data is available. These are targets, not measurements of an app that
already exists. Reference: https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds.
Explicit exclusions
No automated bank verification without a supported verified integration; no live esports game telemetry or anti-cheat
claim; no guaranteed notification delivery; no automatic Instagram vote scraping; no offline scan claiming final success.
Meals, certificates and guest hospitality are optional modules, not promises inherited from Crossfire. The college must
enable and define them before they appear publicly.

Esports
The PDF advertises Kurukshetra, Free Fire and BGMI with a combined esports prize pool of ₹20,000. Organizer
attribution is Srustiprosar, supplied by the user. Entry fee, player count, substitutes, match format, server, device

Each game has a draft event record with registration disabled. Display “Registration details coming soon” until
approved. Do not distribute ₹20,000 equally or advertise ₹56,000 as a confirmed festival-wide total without approval.
Required event rules
All participants register and pay before the activity; roster changes need organizer approval. Schedules, time limits,
conduct and fair play apply. Disruption, cheating, disrespect or property damage can disqualify. Evaluator and
committee decisions are final under the source rules. Prizes require completed activity and verified results.
IPL Auction uses an external evaluator and ranking by points. Budget, player pool, squad size, role constraints and
scoring formula are not specified; publish a versioned rules supplement before play. Recognized bids are binding,
overspending is prohibited and teams explain squad choices.
e-Trivia uses an internal evaluator and one point per correct entrepreneur answer. Quizmaster defines round formats,
answer submission and time limits. Phones, internet and outside help are prohibited unless a round allows them.
Publish whether response time affects score or only ties before the quiz.
Treasure Hunt rewards the first and second teams to solve all clues/checkpoints correctly in the least time. The
discipline award is separate and discretionary, although shown in the third-prize column. Keep teams together unless a
checkpoint allows separation; prohibit unsafe areas, disruption, tampering and unauthorized help. Server timestamps

“Assigned” always means explicit current database assignment, not a frontend route or email suffix. Scope checks
apply to organizer, event, duty window and record ownership on every request. A global role does not remove
conflict-of-interest restrictions.
Account lifecycle
Students verify an email address, complete name, mobile, college, course/year and college-ID number only where
needed for eligibility, and accept terms. No bulk public student list. Captains invite members with expiring single-use
links; each member authenticates and consents. A captain cannot set another person's password or silently consent for
them.
Teachers are invited or submit an access request for manual college verification. Staff invitation is issued to a specific
address and role; first sign-in requires account setup and MFA enrollment. No shared volunteer accounts. Admins may
assign already verified volunteers/judges only within their own events. Only super admins grant admin/finance
privileges. Assignment expiry automatically removes effective permissions.
Provision the first super admin through a documented owner-controlled bootstrap outside public registration. Require a
second named super admin for beneficiary changes and recovery. The last active super admin cannot be demoted
without an approved replacement. There is no public role dropdown that grants elevated access.
Session and audit behavior
Staff MFA is enforced on the server and database, not just shown as a setup prompt. Proposed staff idle timeout: 30
minutes with reauthentication; require recent MFA for privilege grants, exports, refunds and beneficiary changes.
Revoked roles must stop working immediately by checking active membership on each sensitive action; do not wait for
a cached role claim to expire. Student sessions use normal managed-auth limits and explicit sign-out on shared
devices.
Audit entries contain actor, effective role, scope, action, record, time, reason, before/after summary and request
correlation ID. Exclude raw QR tokens, passwords, session tokens and full payment screenshots. Audit access is
restricted and retention follows college policy. Application users cannot update or delete audit records.

Registration payments and QR workflows
Keep registration, payment, eligibility and attendance as distinct records. The UI must describe the real state and
required next action. “Evidence submitted” does not mean “Paid”, and “Paid” does not automatically mean “Eligible to
participate”.
Student and team journey
Browse either organizer track, select an event, read price/size/rules, sign in, complete college profile, choose team or
individual mode where published, obtain member consent, review the roster and fee, accept the event rules version,
reserve a slot, pay the displayed official beneficiary, submit a transaction reference and optional supporting receipt,
await verification, and receive a confirmed event registration and individual pass.
The ₹99 team option covers one valid team. ₹59 individual intake is a separate product, not ₹59 multiplied automatically
into a team fee. Proposed individual policy: coordinator pools individually paid applicants into valid teams with consent,
with no extra ₹99 charge; no pass until allocation completes. This policy needs college approval before individual
checkout opens. Until then show the documented individual price with “Individual registration policy awaiting
confirmation”; do not accept unallocatable payments.
For team mode, enforce 2–4 members or 2–3 for Brand War before payment. Pending invitations must be resolved first.
Once evidence is submitted, freeze the price and roster snapshot. A post-payment roster change requires admin
review, member consent, eligibility recheck, audit and pass reissue for affected members. Moving captainship must not
create a second order or payment.

Proposed hold: 30 minutes to submit evidence. Evidence submitted on time protects the slot through manual review.
Escalate review older than 24 hours to finance; never silently expire a paid-looking submission. Capacity counts
confirmed entries and active holds/reviews; waitlist and abandoned drafts do not count. Exact timer and support
coverage need approval. Allocate the last slot transactionally so two users cannot buy it.
If a transfer arrives after a slot expires or an event fills, mark “Verified payment awaiting resolution”; do not
force-confirm above capacity. Finance offers an approved reallocation or refund. This exception blocks automatic pass
issuance and appears in a dedicated queue.
Payment configuration
The college supplies its official QR image, beneficiary name, UPI identifier and authorized receiving account
confirmation through a controlled channel. Two named approvers verify decoded destination against college records
and conduct an approved test transfer before activation. Store a versioned payment profile with image checksum,
verified destination, effective time and approvals. Never recreate an official QR using guessed information.
Desktop checkout shows the verified QR, beneficiary, exact amount, order reference and a copyable UPI ID. Mobile
also offers an approved UPI intent link if the college provides/verifies the required details, plus QR download/manual
payment guidance. Static QR often does not encode the order amount, so show the exact amount prominently. Do not
assume a UPI app return, intent callback, screenshot or typed UTR proves payment.
Payment credentials are separate from entry-pass QR tokens. Never place the college payment QR on an attendance
pass. Never style a decorative QR as a functional one. The design boards use labelled boxes for missing QR assets.
Reconciliation state machine
Payment progresses from unpaid to evidence_submitted, then under_review, then verified, rejected or
needs_information. Corrections append a new evidence version. Match transaction reference, credited amount,
beneficiary, transaction time and settlement evidence from authorized bank/merchant records. Reviewer records
source/reference and reason. No screen scraping of personal banking accounts is part of this plan.
Use a unique settled bank transaction identifier and receiving account combination to prevent the same credit funding
two orders. Typed UTR uniqueness alone is not proof. Handle duplicated submissions idempotently. Reject a reviewer
verifying their own order or a team they belong to; route to another reviewer. UI displays the verification history without
exposing bank statements to students.
Underpayment, overpayment, missing reference, duplicate reference, wrong beneficiary, unreadable evidence and late
payment each enter explicit exception queues. Do not silently change an order price to match a transfer. One verified
transaction funds one order in the first release; split/combined bank transfers require finance resolution. Refund states
are requested, approved, processing, completed or failed, with amount, reason and actual bank reference; clicking
approve is not proof money was returned. Refund policy and service targets require college approval.
Pass issuance and scanning
A valid confirmed registration issues one pass per authenticated participant per event; the team captain also sees a
roster summary. Each pass has a random opaque token of at least 128 bits, its hash stored server-side, expiry, version,
event and participant association. The QR contains no name, phone, college ID, payment data or role. Display event,
participant display name, team, date/time when published and a human-readable support reference beside the QR.
Volunteer signs in with MFA, starts an assigned shift, selects the authorized station/session, permits camera access
and scans. Server checks token validity, current registration, revocation, event/session, duty assignment and
attendance uniqueness in one transaction. Successful attendance returns a concise green confirmation with matching
identity and time; repeated scan returns amber “Already checked in” with original time; wrong event, invalid or revoked
pass returns a clear denial. Never reveal a full profile for an invalid token.

Treasure checkpoints and optional entitlements
Checkpoint completion is separate from event attendance. Assigned marshals validate a team's pass and checkpoint in
order, recording server time and any approved penalty. The last checkpoint does not automatically publish a winner; the
evaluator reviews correct completion and penalties. Optional meal/certificate records use their own eligibility and
uniqueness rules and remain off until college approval.

Experience and brand specification
The public site should feel like the brochure brought into a clear, fast web experience: charcoal, metallic gold, ember
orange, deep red and warm white. Management screens should emphasize readable data and unambiguous status.
The attached design boards are static concepts, not working screens.
Two event entrances
Desktop hero shows two equal panels simultaneously, with a shared institution header and the event date above them.
Left: “Fire & Flames / The E-Fest / Organized by ED Cell”, six activities, documented ₹36,000 activity pool, “Explore
E-Fest”. Right: “Kurukshetra / Esports / Organized by Srustiprosar”, Free Fire and BGMI, advertised ₹20,000 esports
pool, “Explore Esports”. Unknown fees show “To be announced”. Do not put a single ambiguous Register button over
both panels or hide one track in an auto-rotating carousel.
At 360–430 px, use stacked compact hero panels in a clear order, each with its organizer label and action. Both are
reachable immediately without dismissing an intro. A compact top-level track selector links to each section. Use
separate URLs /e-fest and /esports; individual event pages live below the correct track. Selection persists through
sign-in using a validated internal return path. Site-wide account view groups registrations by organizer.
Avoid compulsory loading animations, scroll hijacking, autoplay sound and heavy WebGL. Optional brief ember
animation starts after content is usable, stops offscreen/in background and honors reduced motion. Deep links, back
navigation and recovery after refresh must work without replaying an intro.

Use consistent JSON errors: code, safe message, field errors and correlation ID. Return 401 for unauthenticated, 403

Transactions and concurrency
Capacity reservation locks the event capacity record and counts holds consistently within a transaction. Confirming
payment atomically records credit allocation, payment review, registration transition, pass records, audit and notification
outbox. Solo intake may become payment-verified while waiting for allocation; only the allocation command can
subsequently confirm it. Attendance inserts use a database uniqueness constraint and idempotency key to tolerate
retries and simultaneous scanners.
Use expected version numbers for admin edits, result approval and payment profile changes. Server rechecks
permissions and state at commit time. Idempotency keys are scoped to actor plus command and bind to a request
payload hash; a reused key with a different payload is rejected. Background work claims jobs atomically and tolerates
duplicate delivery.
Storage and environments
Separate development, staging and production identities, storage and databases. Synthetic data only in tests. Store
uploaded payment evidence privately, with short-lived authorized download URLs, randomized names and content
checks. Public assets contain only approved artwork. Backups and restore permissions belong to named college
owners; deployment rollback must account for forward-only schema migrations. Use append-only migrations and
release-specific restore instructions, never run destructive reset scripts against production.
Proposed service objectives
Target 99.5% availability over the event period, read response p95 below 800 ms and mutation p95 below 1.5 seconds
at approved load, excluding user camera acquisition. Proposed recovery targets are one hour RTO and fifteen minutes
RPO only if the purchased service and tested backup method support them. Otherwise document the measured limits
and obtain a decision before launch. Alerts cover elevated scan failures, review backlog, failed jobs, capacity anomalies
and payment profile changes.

Data minimization and consent
Collect the minimum to identify and contact participants: name, verified email, phone where operationally needed,
college and course/year. College-ID number or visual ID inspection must have a stated eligibility purpose; do not collect
Aadhaar or bank credentials. Keep player IDs only for esports when approved rules require them. Do not collect face
recognition data or continuous location.
Registration terms explain organizer identity, selected event, fees, team-member consent, payment verification,
cancellation/refund policy, conduct, contact and data use. Separate optional publicity/photo consent from required
registration acceptance. Captains cannot give optional consent for teammates. Public results use team/display names
only unless participants have been informed and consent where appropriate.
Proposed retention: payment evidence 90 days after final reconciliation, participant contact records 180 days after
event closure, and audit/financial records according to the college's approved obligations and policy. These are
proposals, not legal retention claims. The college privacy owner must approve retention, deletion requests, dispute
holds and treatment of any under-18 participants before launch. Age eligibility is not stated in the sources; add a gate
rather than invent a minimum age or consent rule.
Privileged changes and recovery
Payment destination changes require two different named super admins and recent MFA. Notify both organizer leads
and finance on activation. Role grants, bulk exports, refunds and result corrections require reasons and are auditable.
Prevent self-approval and changing grants to bypass a review conflict. Staff who also compete may not judge or
reconcile their own event entry.
Recovery uses a named college owner, independent identity verification, recorded reason and session revocation. Do
not email passwords or share recovery codes through event groups. Lost volunteer device: suspend duty and revoke
sessions immediately; reissue access on a verified replacement device. Leaked pass: revoke and issue a new version.
Suspected beneficiary compromise: pause checkout immediately, preserve logs, verify account with college finance,
notify affected participants using approved communication.
Incident runbook
Detect and record an incident ID, severity and owner. Contain by revoking sessions, pausing affected workflow or
disabling a track. Preserve relevant audit/evidence without broad data export. Assess affected records, recover from
tested backups if needed, notify authorized college leads, and communicate factually to affected users under college
policy. Record resolution and preventative action. A frontend rollback alone does not undo financial or database
changes.
Release security gate
No production launch with a known privilege escalation, cross-scope data leak, duplicate-credit allocation, invalid pass
acceptance, exposed secret or unrecoverable data loss. Require reviewed policies for every private table/storage
bucket, a secret scan, dependency review, role tests and an incident rehearsal. Do not promise penetration-test
certification; commission an independent review if required by the college.

Notification design and message catalogue
Use durable in-app messages as the primary record and verified email for transactional alerts. SMS/WhatsApp are

operational. Never call a message delivered merely because an API accepted it.
Delivery rules
Write the event and outbox row in the same database transaction as the business action. Worker sends at least once; a
deduplication key of business event, recipient, template version and channel prevents repeated user-facing sends.
Save queued, attempted, accepted, delivered where a provider proves it, bounced and failed separately. Track in-app

Proposed email retry schedule: 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes and 2 hours for transient failures; permanent failures
go directly to the support queue. Honor provider rate limits. Staff dashboard alerts on exhausted attempts; a retried
notification must not repeat the underlying payment or registration action. Cancellation suppresses obsolete scheduled
reminders. Send one current schedule change summary instead of every intermediate draft.
